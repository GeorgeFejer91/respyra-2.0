Default recorded variables

This reference describes a fresh Respyra Suite v0.3.8 installation, bundling Polar and Vernier Stream Mini v0.6.6. It covers every default signal channel and acquisition diagnostic; copied channels are defined once and their stream locations are listed below.

Installations publish no physiological data without connected, streaming sensors. Saved preferences survive upgrades; selected outputs may remain empty during warmup, insufficient cycles or a disconnected sensor, and recording starts when you press Start.

Polar Stream Mini

The fresh selection includes 4 direct outputs and 33 Breathing/Breathing dynamics metrics, plus All-in-one. The app also publishes its continuity-marker outlet; raw ACC has three channels, and every derived metric has a scalar outlet.

ACC-derived respiration estimates motion from a chest-mounted H10. The signed candidates share PCA axis learning; they are algorithm variants, not independent respiratory sensors or validated airflow/volume measurements. Dynamics retain up to 256 accepted entries: features need at least 8, while complexity/entropy computation needs 24. Below 24 entries the emitted complexity/entropy values are zero placeholders, not evidence of perfectly regular breathing.

  • Raw ECGraw_ecg · Polar-H10-Mini_rawECG

    The H10 forwards its single-lead voltage samples in microvolts (µV), at a configured 130 Hz, without Mini-derived feature extraction. This waveform supports independent cardiac processing and signal inspection; it is not a diagnostic ECG.

  • Raw accelerometer Xraw_acc · X · Polar-H10-Mini_rawACC

    The H10’s X-axis acceleration is forwarded in milligravity (mg; 1,000 mg = 1 g), at a configured 200 Hz, as one channel of the raw three-axis ACC outlet. It includes gravity, posture and body movement as well as small breathing-related motion, so this axis alone is not a respiratory volume signal.

  • Raw accelerometer Yraw_acc · Y · Polar-H10-Mini_rawACC

    The H10’s Y-axis acceleration is forwarded in milligravity (mg; 1,000 mg = 1 g), at a configured 200 Hz, as one channel of the raw three-axis ACC outlet. It includes gravity, posture and body movement as well as small breathing-related motion, so this axis alone is not a respiratory volume signal.

  • Raw accelerometer Zraw_acc · Z · Polar-H10-Mini_rawACC

    The H10’s Z-axis acceleration is forwarded in milligravity (mg; 1,000 mg = 1 g), at a configured 200 Hz, as one channel of the raw three-axis ACC outlet. It includes gravity, posture and body movement as well as small breathing-related motion, so this axis alone is not a respiratory volume signal.

  • Heart rateheart_rate · Polar-H10-Mini_heartRate

    The H10 reports its detected cardiac beat rate in beats per minute (bpm) through the Bluetooth heart-rate characteristic. It is a device-derived rate, not a Mini estimate from the recorded ECG or a specific measure of emotion.

  • RR intervalrr_interval · Polar-H10-Mini_rrInterval

    The H10 reports the time between successive detected heartbeats in milliseconds (ms), decoded from Bluetooth RR values. These intervals support cardiac timing and later HRV analysis after artifact screening; they are not respiratory-cycle intervals.

  • ADR PCA waveformadr_pca_waveform · Polar-H10-Mini_adrPcaWaveform

    A smoothed, centered projection of raw ACC (mg converted to g) onto a fixed principal axis learned during the initial calibration, expressed in g. It retains signed chest motion and is a selectable Respyra input, but its mathematical sign needs an inhale-direction choice and its amplitude is not lung volume.

  • ADR PCA relative amplitudeadr_pca_relative_amplitude · Polar-H10-Mini_adrPcaRelativeAmplitude

    The PCA projection is rescaled between robust output bounds and clipped to a dimensionless 0–1 range. This expresses relative chest-motion amplitude; bounds can adapt, so the value is neither a fixed physical displacement nor Respyra’s study calibration.

  • ADR axis-difference breath eventadr_axis_difference_event · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAxisDifferenceEvent

    An event sample of 1 is emitted when the rectified sum of per-axis 0.2 s minus 200 s ACC means crosses an adaptive threshold with quiet-time and refractory gates. Its unit is an event count, and each detection is a candidate breath onset from a chest-mounted adaptation of Phan’s phone algorithm.

  • ADR axis-difference respiratory rateadr_axis_difference_rate · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAxisDifferenceRate

    The number of axis-difference breath events in the preceding 60 s is reported in breaths/min. It is a rolling detector count whose false or missed events can reflect posture and motion, rather than a validated reference respiratory rate.

  • ADR moving-average respiratory phaseadr_moving_average_phase · Polar-H10-Mini_adrMovingAveragePhase

    The signed PCA waveform’s short-minus-long mean contrast, divided by its calibrated span, is classified as +1 rising/inhale, −1 falling/exhale, 0 pause, or −2 bad/unready signal. These dimensionless class codes use exploratory Flowborne-style thresholds (+0.025/−0.075); they describe inferred movement phase rather than measured airflow.

  • ADR moving-average difference waveformadr_moving_average_difference · Polar-H10-Mini_adrMovingAverageDifference

    The 0.267 s mean minus the 2 s mean of the signed PCA projection is divided by the calibrated axis span, producing a dimensionless ratio. This continuous Flowborne-style motion contrast has frequency-dependent shape and delay, and its validity companion distinguishes diagnostic samples from usable intervals.

  • ADR moving-average validityadr_moving_average_valid · Polar-H10-Mini_adrMovingAverageValid

    A dimensionless 0/1 flag indicates usable PCA input, a filled 2 s moving-average window, and an absolute contrast no greater than one calibrated span. Zero marks an unusable or filling estimator, not a physiological breath hold.

  • ADR signed axis-mean difference waveformadr_axis_mean_difference · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAxisMeanDifference

    The signed projection of per-axis 0.2 s minus 200 s ACC means onto the shared PCA axis is reported in g. This Phan-window adaptation preserves direction and is a selectable Respyra input; the long baseline fills progressively, and both axis-difference and PCA validity flags apply.

  • ADR rectified axis-difference waveformadr_axis_difference_magnitude · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAxisDifferenceMagnitude

    The sum of absolute per-axis differences between 0.2 s and 200 s ACC means is reported in g. This continuous rectified Phan score discards direction, can produce excursions during both halves of a breath, and is not a signed inhalation waveform.

  • ADR axis-difference validityadr_axis_difference_valid · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAxisDifferenceValid

    A dimensionless 0/1 flag reports whether at least 0.2 s of contiguous accepted ACC samples have filled the short window since the last gap or clock reset. The 200 s baseline may still be filling, and the signed waveform additionally needs PCA validity.

  • ADR PCA signal quality indexadr_pca_quality · Polar-H10-Mini_adrPcaQuality

    A dimensionless 0–1 index combines calibrated-range quality, all-axis motion quality and PCA dominance in the fresh timed-PCA processor, returning zero when unready. It is an app-specific signal-quality heuristic, not the probability that the waveform correctly measures breathing.

  • ADR PCA validityadr_pca_valid · Polar-H10-Mini_adrPcaValid

    A dimensionless 0/1 processing flag requires completed PCA calibration, fresh samples and acceptable motion quality (at least 0.35). It marks readiness for using the projection, not validated physiological accuracy.

  • ADR PCA respiratory phaseadr_pca_phase · Polar-H10-Mini_adrPcaPhase

    A smoothed derivative of the fixed-calibration PCA projection is classified with hysteresis and dwell rules into +1 inhale/rising, −1 exhale/falling, or 0 pause/not ready/bad signal. These dimensionless motion classes depend on mounting and axis sign; consult PCA validity to distinguish an unusable signal from a pause.

  • Breathing calibrationadr_pca_calibration · Polar-H10-Mini_adrPcaCalibration

    Accepted accelerometer source time divided by the configured calibration duration gives a dimensionless 0–1 progress value. Reaching 1 indicates that the calibration interval elapsed, while waveform readiness still depends on sufficient movement range and quality.

  • Breathing axis rangeadr_pca_range · Polar-H10-Mini_adrPcaRange

    The difference between the PCA output’s robust upper and lower bounds is reported in g. It describes the acceleration span used for relative scaling; either breathing or unrelated movement can enlarge it.

  • Breathing rateadr_pca_rate · Polar-H10-Mini_adrPcaRate

    The app converts the mean interval between accepted same-polarity PCA extrema to breaths/min as 60 divided by mean interval in seconds. The estimate needs accepted cycles and can be distorted by movement, shallow breathing or missed extrema.

  • Breathing-dynamics confidenceadr_dynamics_quality · Polar-H10-Mini_adrDynamicsQuality

    The larger accepted interval/amplitude count divided by 200, clipped to 0–1 and multiplied by current PCA quality gives a dimensionless score. It combines accumulated data sufficiency with signal quality, rather than confidence in a clinical or emotional interpretation.

  • Breath interval meanadr_interval_mean · Polar-H10-Mini_adrIntervalMean

    The arithmetic mean of accepted same-polarity extremum intervals is reported in seconds (s). It summarizes typical detected cycle duration.

  • Breath interval SDadr_interval_sd · Polar-H10-Mini_adrIntervalSD

    The sample standard deviation of accepted cycle durations is reported in seconds (s). It describes absolute interval variability, which can arise from breathing changes or artifacts.

  • Breath interval CVadr_interval_cv · Polar-H10-Mini_adrIntervalCV

    The standard deviation divided by the absolute mean of accepted cycle durations gives a dimensionless ratio. It describes relative interval variability and has no universal healthy-breathing threshold.

  • Breath interval ACW50adr_interval_acw50 · Polar-H10-Mini_adrIntervalACW50

    The first lag at which the accepted interval sequence’s autocorrelation falls below 0.5 is reported with LSL unit breaths, operationally a count of sequence entries. Peak-to-peak and trough-to-trough intervals are interleaved, so the lag is not directly seconds or a count of complete respiratory cycles.

  • Breath interval PSD slopeadr_interval_psd_slope · Polar-H10-Mini_adrIntervalPsdSlope

    An ordinary-least-squares slope of log power against log frequency is calculated from the detrended, standardized accepted interval sequence. The slope is dimensionless and exploratory, with sensitivity to record length, artifacts and spectral estimation.

  • Breath interval Lempel–Zivadr_interval_lzc · Polar-H10-Mini_adrIntervalLZC

    The accepted interval sequence is binarized around its mean, and a normalized Lempel–Ziv phrase count is reported in nominal 0–1 complexity units. Larger values indicate a less compressible binary pattern, without establishing respiratory health or emotional flexibility.

  • Breath interval sample entropyadr_interval_sampen · Polar-H10-Mini_adrIntervalSampEn

    Sample entropy of accepted interval values is −ln(A/B), with pattern length 2, tolerance 0.2 times SD and delay 1, in dimensionless entropy units. It describes pattern unpredictability; this release emits zero before 24 entries or when the estimator is undefined, so zero needs a data-sufficiency check.

  • Breath interval multiscale entropyadr_interval_mse · Polar-H10-Mini_adrIntervalMSE

    Sample entropy with pattern length 3, delay 1 and tolerance 0.2 times the original interval SD is integrated over usable coarse-graining scales 1–5, in dimensionless entropy-AUC units. This exploratory implementation omits undefined scales and returns zero before 24 entries or without enough valid scales; zero is not a validated regularity score.

  • Breath amplitude meanadr_amplitude_mean · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAmplitudeMean

    The arithmetic mean of accepted alternating peak-to-trough excursions in the normalized PCA waveform is reported in dimensionless 0–1 amplitude units. It summarizes relative chest-motion excursion, not tidal volume.

  • Breath amplitude SDadr_amplitude_sd · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAmplitudeSD

    The sample standard deviation of accepted normalized peak-to-trough excursions is reported in dimensionless amplitude units. It describes absolute amplitude variability, which can arise from breathing changes or artifacts.

  • Breath amplitude CVadr_amplitude_cv · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAmplitudeCV

    The standard deviation divided by the absolute mean of accepted normalized breath excursions gives a dimensionless ratio. It describes relative amplitude variability and has no universal healthy-breathing threshold.

  • Breath amplitude ACW50adr_amplitude_acw50 · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAmplitudeACW50

    The first lag at which the accepted amplitude sequence’s autocorrelation falls below 0.5 is reported with LSL unit breaths, operationally a count of sequence entries. Both peak-to-trough and trough-to-peak excursions enter the sequence, so the lag is not directly seconds or a count of complete respiratory cycles.

  • Breath amplitude PSD slopeadr_amplitude_psd_slope · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAmplitudePsdSlope

    An ordinary-least-squares slope of log power against log frequency is calculated from the detrended, standardized accepted amplitude sequence. The slope is dimensionless and exploratory, with sensitivity to record length, artifacts and spectral estimation.

  • Breath amplitude Lempel–Zivadr_amplitude_lzc · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAmplitudeLZC

    The accepted amplitude sequence is binarized around its mean, and a normalized Lempel–Ziv phrase count is reported in nominal 0–1 complexity units. Larger values indicate a less compressible binary pattern, without establishing respiratory health or emotional flexibility.

  • Breath amplitude sample entropyadr_amplitude_sampen · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAmplitudeSampEn

    Sample entropy of accepted amplitude values is −ln(A/B), with pattern length 2, tolerance 0.2 times SD and delay 1, in dimensionless entropy units. It describes pattern unpredictability; this release emits zero before 24 entries or when the estimator is undefined, so zero needs a data-sufficiency check.

  • Breath amplitude multiscale entropyadr_amplitude_mse · Polar-H10-Mini_adrAmplitudeMSE

    Sample entropy with pattern length 3, delay 1 and tolerance 0.2 times the original amplitude SD is integrated over usable coarse-graining scales 1–5, in dimensionless entropy-AUC units. This exploratory implementation omits undefined scales and returns zero before 24 entries or without enough valid scales; zero is not a validated regularity score.

  • Polar signal statePolar-H10-Mini-signalStatus · signal_state

    The continuity marker contains code 1 for Bluetooth signal lost and code 2 for restored, emitted on transitions rather than at a fixed rate. This dimensionless transport state says nothing about physiological quality and does not synthesize samples during a gap.

  • Polar combined-row event kindPolar-H10-Mini · event_kind

    The All-in-one outlet tags sparse rows with code 1 ECG, 2 ACC, 3 HR/RR, 10 metric snapshot, 20 signal lost or 21 restored. This dimensionless routing code identifies the row’s payload; other signal columns are NaN when that signal did not update.

Vernier Stream Mini

All eight output choices start selected. The supported GDX-RB exposes Force, Respiration Rate, Steps and Step Rate; the complete raw outlet also includes the seven diagnostics below. Other metadata-exposed channels are device-dependent and keep their declared label and unit.

  • ForceForce · Vernier-GDX-Mini_rawVernier; copy: _rawForce

    The belt’s force transducer measures belt tension in newtons (N), forwarded from the Go Direct measurement channel without Mini normalization. Increasing force is associated with inhalation when correctly fitted, but absolute tension depends on placement and preload and is not airflow or tidal volume.

  • Respiration RateRespiration Rate · _rawVernier; copy: _respirationRate

    The GDX-RB firmware estimates breathing frequency from belt force and reports breaths per minute (device unit bpm). Vernier specifies a 30 s calculation window and 10 s update interval, so this device-derived estimate is distinct from Polar’s detectors and instantaneous force.

  • StepsSteps · _rawVernier; copy: _steps

    The belt’s firmware reports a cumulative count from its built-in pedometer, in steps. Mini forwards the count without zeroing it, so it may include activity preceding the session and is not a raw accelerometer axis.

  • Step RateStep Rate · _rawVernier; copy: _stepRate

    The belt’s firmware estimates walking cadence in steps per minute (spm). Vernier specifies a 10 s calculation window and 10 s update interval, so this is a device-derived activity rate.

  • Vernier breathing waveformVernier-GDX-Mini_vernierBreathing · Vernier breathing waveform

    Mini maps force into a clipped, dimensionless 0–1 waveform using a rolling 30 s range, normally the 5th and 95th percentiles after warmup. Higher values retain the increasing-force direction; this adaptive relative-tension signal is separate from Respyra calibration, holds its last output for nonfinite force and defaults to 0.5 for a degenerate range.

  • Vernier signal stateVernier-GDX-Mini-signalStatus · signal_state

    The continuity marker contains code 1 for Bluetooth signal lost and 2 for restored, with the same code also present in combined marker rows. This dimensionless connection indicator marks acquisition gaps, rather than breath pauses or sensor accuracy.

  • Raw row sequence_rawVernier / All-in-one · sequence

    A monotonically advancing, dimensionless row count identifies each decoded raw measurement row. Discontinuities help audit transport loss or restarts; the counter is not elapsed time.

  • Dropped rows before_rawVernier / All-in-one · dropped_rows_before

    The number of raw rows dropped by the bounded host input queue before the current batch is reported in rows. The count is attached to its first row and zero to remaining rows, so sum it across rows rather than treating every row as a fresh loss report.

  • Device drop reports before_rawVernier / All-in-one · device_drop_reports_before

    The number of device-level dropped-packet reports observed before this batch is reported as a dimensionless count. It is attached to the batch’s first row and is a report count, not necessarily the exact number of missing samples.

  • Configured sample period_rawVernier / All-in-one · sample_period_us

    The configured interval used to backfill periodic rows is recorded in microseconds (µs), with zero meaning no interval. The standard Mini request is 50,000 µs (20 Hz), but device metadata and actual sparse updates govern interpretation.

  • Decode latency_rawVernier / All-in-one · decode_latency_ns

    Host notification-to-decode latency is recorded in nanoseconds (ns). It measures software handling after reception, not the entire sensor-to-display latency or the physiological onset time.

  • Host receive timestamp_rawVernier / All-in-one · host_receive_timestamp_ns

    The notification’s monotonic receive time since the native measurement-session origin is recorded in nanoseconds (ns). It supports acquisition diagnostics and is a separate clock from the LSL/XDF sample timestamp.

  • Device encoding code_rawVernier / All-in-one · encoding_code

    A dimensionless code identifies a Float32 device frame as 0 and an Int32 frame as 1. The raw recording stores both losslessly in Double64 rows; the code describes representation, not a measurement.

Device rate/count definitions: Vernier GDX-RB manual. Transformation and diagnostic definitions: Mini force processor and recorded channel metadata.

Respyra 2.0

One selected input drives the task: Vernier raw Force, Polar PCA waveform, or Polar signed axis-mean difference. Respyra adds the selected calibrated waveform, separate comparison copies for usable alternatives and study events; comparison recording starts On in fresh settings.

  • Calibrated breathing — selected inputRespyra-Calibrated-Breathing · Calibrated breathing

    Respyra records (Force − center)/amplitude for Vernier input, or (polarity × waveform − center)/amplitude for selected Polar input, using the accepted study range. The output is dimensionless, unclipped and independent of feedback gain; NaN precedes calibration, 0 marks center and ±1 marks the scaled half-range, not physical volume.

  • Calibrated breathing — each alternativeRespyra-Comparison-<source hash> · Calibrated breathing

    With default comparison recording enabled, each other live compatible input gets its own (signal − center)/amplitude waveform from samples collected during the same accepted range attempt. These dimensionless comparison copies can represent Vernier force, Polar PCA or signed Phan, retain native Polar +1 polarity and NaN until usable calibration, and never drive participant feedback.

  • Respyra study eventsRespyra-Events · one JSON string channel

    Each irregular LSL sample is a JSON event containing its name, run UUID, sequence, LSL time and trial/condition/phase/screen context, plus event-specific values. Seconds apply to timestamps and durations while other fields carry declared units or categories; markers describe setup, calibration, task transitions, responses and recorder lifecycle rather than a continuous physiological waveform.

These are JSON fields within Respyra-Events, rather than extra scalar LSL outlets. Event-specific payloads also retain calibration center/amplitude and polarity, target segments/gain, participant metadata, responses and recording receipts.

Additional task CSV variables

These are saved automatically by Respyra’s sample and assessment loggers, alongside XDF. They are task-table fields, not additional default LSL outlets; trial and condition also appear in event JSON.

  • Task elapsed timetimestamp

    The task logger stores the PsychoPy phase clock’s elapsed time in seconds, rounded to four decimals in tracking rows. This is phase-relative software time, rather than an absolute sensor timestamp; use XDF for cross-stream timing.

  • Frame counterframe

    The current study-loop frame counter is recorded as a dimensionless integer. Several input samples can be logged on one frame, so this identifies task processing context rather than a unique sensor sample or physical display onset.

  • Recorded task inputforce_n / signal_g

    The received selected input is logged in N for Vernier force or g for the signed, polarity-adjusted Polar waveform, rounded to four decimals in tracking rows. It remains in native signal units, while the separate calibrated LSL output is dimensionless.

  • Target signaltarget_force / target_signal_g

    The generated sinusoidal target uses the study’s calibrated center, amplitude and configured segments, in N for Vernier or g for Polar. It is the reference participants are asked to track, rather than a measured physiological signal.

  • Uncompensated tracking errorerror / error_g

    The target minus the selected input is stored in N for Vernier or g for Polar. This signed difference quantifies tracking in native input units before visual gain is applied.

  • Gain-compensated tracking errorcompensated_error / compensated_error_g

    The target minus the displayed signal, center + gain × (input − center), is stored in N for Vernier or g for Polar. This signed difference describes error under the visual manipulation; trial performance averages its absolute value.

  • Task phasephase

    A categorical string identifies the task stage attached to a sample, such as range_cal, baseline, countdown or tracking. It has no physical unit and separates experimental periods for analysis.

  • Experimental conditioncondition

    A categorical label identifies the configured normal, deep, shallow or corresponding no-feedback condition. It has no physical unit and describes the task manipulation, not an independently measured breathing-depth class.

  • Trial numbertrial_num

    The one-based trial index is recorded as a dimensionless count, with no active trial during session-level calibration. It links sample rows and assessment responses to the configured order.

  • Visual feedback gainfeedback_gain

    The condition’s dimensionless gain scales input deviations about the calibrated center, with 1.0 normal, 0.5 attenuated and 1.5 amplified feedback. This is an experimental parameter rather than a sensor measure, and does not rescale the calibrated LSL channel.

  • Perceived breathing conditionself_condition

    The participant’s key response records n for normal, d for deeper or s for shallower breathing needed to stay on target. It is a categorical, unitless self-report after the trial, distinct from the assigned condition.

  • Judgment confidenceconfidence

    The participant rates confidence in the breathing judgment using a key from 1 (completely unsure) to 5 (sure). It is an ordinal self-report scale without a physical unit.

  • Perceived tracking accuracyself_accuracy

    The participant rates the preceding trial from 1 (very inaccurate) to 5 (very accurate). It is an ordinal, unitless self-report rather than the objective tracking-error measure.

Default stream inventory and copies

An LSL stream is a container; a variable is one channel or JSON field inside it. The raw, scalar and combined copies below do not constitute independent measurements. Default base names can be renamed in Mini settings; stream metadata and source_id identify the actual producer.

  • Polar: Polar-H10-Mini_<suffix> for each of the 37 selected metric IDs above; rawACC contains X/Y/Z. Polar-H10-Mini-signalStatus adds continuity codes.
  • Polar All-in-one: Polar-H10-Mini contains event_kind, raw_ecg_uv, acc_x_mg, acc_y_mg, acc_z_mg, heart_rate_bpm, rr_interval_ms and the 33 selected ADR suffix columns.
  • Vernier: Vernier-GDX-Mini_rawVernier contains device channels and seven diagnostics; _rawForce, _steps, _stepRate and _respirationRate copy their device values. _vernierBreathing and -signalStatus carry the derived waveform and connection codes.
  • Vernier All-in-one: Vernier-GDX-Mini contains the raw device channels as sensor_<number>_<label>, seven diagnostics, vernier_breathing_01 and signal_state. In this release it bundles raw data, breathing and status, while the four scalar device copies remain separate.
  • Respyra: Respyra-Calibrated-Breathing, Respyra-Comparison-<hash> per usable alternative, and Respyra-Events (the marker name is editable before recording).

With both supported devices streaming and all three inputs usable, this topology can advertise 51 outlets: 39 Polar, 8 Vernier and 4 Respyra. This is an intended inventory, not a guaranteed recording count: availability, preferences, other LSL producers, late discovery and failed comparison calibration change what is present.

Each channel sample also has an LSL/XDF timestamp in seconds, separate from its value. Polar ECG and raw ACC are configured at 130 and 200 Hz; ADR waveforms emit at accepted-notification timing with nominal rate 0 (irregular), and firmware rates/events can be sparse. Combined rows contain NaN for signals that did not update; missing or pre-calibration values are not zero physiology.

Respyra records every visible and later-discovered outlet by default, unless its Record box is unchecked before Start; the selected input, selected calibrated output and study events are required. Discovery cannot recover samples sent before subscription. Extra optional ECG/HRV/coherence features and OS keyboard/mouse hooks are not default selections.

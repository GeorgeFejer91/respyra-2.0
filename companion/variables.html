Default recorded variables

This reference describes the default streams in Respyra Suite v2.3.15 and Stream Mini v0.6.11. The standard Polar selection contains seven signal types, with all three accelerometer axes kept together. Breathing quality and validity are recorded as channels inside their waveform streams; the Vernier and Respyra defaults remain documented below.

Installations publish no physiological data without connected, streaming sensors. Suite installation, upgrade and reinstallation reset stream selections to these defaults and back up the old Mini preferences. Device preferences and recordings are retained. Selected outputs may remain empty during warmup, insufficient cycles or a disconnected sensor, and recording starts when you press Start.

Polar Stream Mini — seven study signals

Fresh settings select exactly these seven signal types. ECG, accelerometer, heart rate and RR intervals come directly from the H10; Mini computes two continuous breathing candidates and one phase classifier. Every selected outlet starts checked for recording in Respyra.

Respyra identifies these inputs by floating-point format, g units, signed-waveform metadata and live validity flags, independently of stream names. Chest Motion and Chest Motion DT are the two supported Polar input contracts. They share the same chest-motion sensor and PCA axis; their units are acceleration, and neither is a validated measurement of airflow or lung volume.

  • ECGPolar_rawECG

    The H10 supplies single-lead voltage samples in microvolts (µV), configured at 130 Hz, without Mini-derived feature extraction. This raw waveform supports independent cardiac processing and signal inspection.

  • AccelerometerPolar_rawACC · X / Y / Z

    The H10 supplies three raw acceleration channels (X, Y and Z) in milligravity (mg; 1,000 mg = 1 g), configured at 200 Hz. These contain gravity, posture, movement and breathing-related chest motion; the two breathing candidates below are calculated from these same axes.

  • Heart ratePolar_HR

    The H10 natively reports its current heart-rate estimate in beats per minute (bpm) over Bluetooth. Mini forwards the device value without deriving a second heart-rate estimate from ECG or RR.

  • RR intervalsPolar_RR

    The H10 natively reports elapsed time between successive cardiac R peaks, in milliseconds (ms). Mini forwards each reported interval; shorter intervals mean faster cardiac timing, and these values support later beat-to-beat or HRV analysis.

  • Chest MotionPolar_ChestPCA

    Mini converts raw acceleration to g and projects centered, smoothed chest motion onto a learned, fixed principal axis. This signed waveform is one of Respyra’s two supported Polar feedback inputs; its amplitude reflects relative chest motion, rather than airflow or lung volume.

  • Chest Motion DTPolar_ChestDT

    DT means detrended: Mini subtracts each acceleration axis’s rolling 200 s baseline from its 0.2 s mean and projects the differences onto the same learned PCA axis, in g. This signed adaptation of Lyn Phan’s method is Respyra’s other supported Polar input; it gradually removes sustained offsets and uses available baseline samples during startup.

  • Estimated breath phasePolar_BreathPhase

    Mini adapts the Flowborne/Viscereality moving-average classifier to signed H10 chest motion and emits a unitless phase code: +1 inhalation, −1 exhalation, 0 pause, or −2 unavailable/bad signal. It records estimated breath phase and cannot drive Respyra feedback; orientation and movement affect its interpretation.

Bundled quality channels and connection events

ChestPCA contains PCA quality and validity. ChestDT contains DT validity, PCA validity and PCA quality. These channels share their waveform sample’s timestamp and are recorded inside its stream. Separate scalar copies remain optional. Polar_Link is the dedicated Bluetooth housekeeping stream, recorded separately by default.

  • PCA qualityPCAQuality · channel in ChestPCA and ChestDT

    A dimensionless 0–1 index combines calibrated-range quality, all-axis motion quality and PCA dominance in the fresh timed-PCA processor, returning zero when unready. It is an app-specific signal-quality heuristic, not the probability that the waveform correctly measures breathing.

  • PCA validityPCAValid · channel in ChestPCA and ChestDT

    A dimensionless 0/1 processing flag requires completed PCA calibration, fresh samples and acceptable motion quality (at least 0.35). It marks readiness for using the projection, not validated physiological accuracy.

  • Phan validityDTValid · channel in ChestDT

    A dimensionless 0/1 flag reports whether at least 0.2 s of contiguous accepted ACC samples have filled the short window since the last gap or clock reset. The 200 s baseline may still be filling, and the signed waveform additionally needs PCA validity.

  • Polar signal statePolar_Link · signal_state

    The applet reports code 1 for Bluetooth signal lost and code 2 for restored. Respyra records Polar_Link directly with its source identity and event timestamps. The paired loss/recovery times describe the observed outage duration. Connection state does not establish physiological quality or synthesize samples during a gap.

All-in-one, alternative waveforms, additional phase/rate estimators, breathing dynamics and ECG/HRV features remain available through Add more metrics, with their checkboxes off by default. Selections made after installation persist until the next suite installation; Use study defaults also restores this minimal selection.

Vernier Stream Mini

Two outlets start selected: raw device data and connection events. The supported GDX-RB exposes Force, Respiration Rate, Steps and Step Rate as channels within the raw outlet, alongside the seven diagnostics below. The six other output choices are optional. Other metadata-exposed channels are device-dependent and keep their declared label and unit.

  • ForceForce · Vernier_rawForce; optional copy: Vernier_rawForceOnly

    The belt’s force transducer measures belt tension in newtons (N), forwarded from the Go Direct measurement channel without Mini normalization. Increasing force is associated with inhalation when correctly fitted, but absolute tension depends on placement and preload and is not airflow or tidal volume.

  • Respiration RateRespiration Rate · Vernier_rawForce; optional copy: _respirationRate

    The GDX-RB firmware estimates breathing frequency from belt force and reports breaths per minute (device unit bpm). Vernier specifies a 30 s calculation window and 10 s update interval, so this device-derived estimate is distinct from Polar’s detectors and instantaneous force.

  • StepsSteps · Vernier_rawForce; optional copy: _steps

    The belt’s firmware reports a cumulative count from its built-in pedometer, in steps. Mini forwards the count without zeroing it, so it may include activity preceding the session and is not a raw accelerometer axis.

  • Step RateStep Rate · Vernier_rawForce; optional copy: _stepRate

    The belt’s firmware estimates walking cadence in steps per minute (spm). Vernier specifies a 10 s calculation window and 10 s update interval, so this is a device-derived activity rate.

  • Vernier signal stateVernier_Link · signal_state

    The applet reports code 1 for Bluetooth signal lost and 2 for restored. Respyra records Vernier_Link directly with its source identity and event timestamps. The paired loss/recovery times describe the observed outage duration. These are connection diagnostics, rather than breath pauses or sensor accuracy.

  • Raw row sequenceVernier_rawForce / All-in-one · sequence

    A monotonically advancing, dimensionless row count identifies each decoded raw measurement row. Discontinuities help audit transport loss or restarts; the counter is not elapsed time.

  • Dropped rows beforeVernier_rawForce / All-in-one · dropped_rows_before

    The number of raw rows dropped by the bounded host input queue before the current batch is reported in rows. The count is attached to its first row and zero to remaining rows, so sum it across rows rather than treating every row as a fresh loss report.

  • Device drop reports beforeVernier_rawForce / All-in-one · device_drop_reports_before

    The number of device-level dropped-packet reports observed before this batch is reported as a dimensionless count. It is attached to the batch’s first row and is a report count, not necessarily the exact number of missing samples.

  • Configured sample periodVernier_rawForce / All-in-one · sample_period_us

    The configured interval used to backfill periodic rows is recorded in microseconds (µs), with zero meaning no interval. The standard Mini request is 50,000 µs (20 Hz), but device metadata and actual sparse updates govern interpretation.

  • Decode latencyVernier_rawForce / All-in-one · decode_latency_ns

    Host notification-to-decode latency is recorded in nanoseconds (ns). It measures software handling after reception, not the entire sensor-to-display latency or the physiological onset time.

  • Host receive timestampVernier_rawForce / All-in-one · host_receive_timestamp_ns

    The notification’s monotonic receive time since the native measurement-session origin is recorded in nanoseconds (ns). It supports acquisition diagnostics and is a separate clock from the LSL/XDF sample timestamp.

  • Device encoding codeVernier_rawForce / All-in-one · encoding_code

    A dimensionless code identifies a Float32 device frame as 0 and an Int32 frame as 1. The raw recording stores both losslessly in Double64 rows; the code describes representation, not a measurement.

Optional Vernier outputs

Raw Force, respiration rate, steps and step-rate copies, the processed breathing waveform and All-in-one start unchecked. The four device values remain available inside the default raw row. Selecting optional outputs adds outlets without changing the study's raw Force input.

  • Vernier breathing waveformVernier_vernierBreathing · Vernier breathing waveform

    Mini maps force into a clipped, dimensionless 0–1 waveform using a rolling 30 s range, normally the 5th and 95th percentiles after warmup. Higher values retain the increasing-force direction; this adaptive relative-tension signal is separate from Respyra calibration, holds its last output for nonfinite force and defaults to 0.5 for a degenerate range.

Device rate/count definitions: Vernier GDX-RB manual. Transformation and diagnostic definitions: Mini force processor and recorded channel metadata.

Respyra 2.0

One selected input drives the task: Vernier raw Force, Chest Motion, or Chest Motion DT. Respyra adds the selected calibrated waveform, separate comparison copies for usable alternatives, study events; comparison recording starts On in fresh settings.

  • Calibrated breathing — selected inputRespyra_<input name>_calibrated · Calibrated breathing

    Respyra records (Force − center)/amplitude for Vernier input, or (polarity × waveform − center)/amplitude for selected Polar input, using the accepted study range. The output is dimensionless, unclipped and independent of feedback gain; NaN precedes calibration, 0 marks center and ±1 marks the scaled half-range, not physical volume.

  • Calibrated breathing — each alternativeRespyra_<alternative input name>_calibrated · Calibrated breathing

    With default comparison recording enabled, each other live compatible input gets its own (signal − center)/amplitude waveform from samples collected during the same accepted range attempt. These dimensionless comparison copies can represent Vernier force, Polar PCA or signed Phan, retain native Polar +1 polarity and NaN until usable calibration, and never drive participant feedback.

  • Respyra study eventsRespyra-Events · one JSON string channel

    Each irregular LSL sample is a JSON event containing its name, run UUID, sequence, LSL time and trial/condition/phase/screen context, plus event-specific values. Seconds apply to timestamps and durations while other fields carry declared units or categories; markers describe setup, calibration, task transitions, responses and recorder lifecycle rather than a continuous physiological waveform.

Fields inside the event channel

These are JSON fields within Respyra-Events, rather than extra scalar LSL outlets. Event-specific payloads also retain calibration center/amplitude and polarity, target segments/gain, participant metadata, responses and recording receipts.

Additional task CSV variables

These are saved automatically by Respyra’s sample and assessment loggers, alongside XDF. They are task-table fields, not additional default LSL outlets; trial and condition also appear in event JSON.

  • Task elapsed timetimestamp

    The task logger stores the PsychoPy phase clock’s elapsed time in seconds, rounded to four decimals in tracking rows. This is phase-relative software time, rather than an absolute sensor timestamp; use XDF for cross-stream timing.

  • Frame counterframe

    The current study-loop frame counter is recorded as a dimensionless integer. Several input samples can be logged on one frame, so this identifies task processing context rather than a unique sensor sample or physical display onset.

  • Recorded task inputforce_n / signal_g

    The received selected input is logged in N for Vernier force or g for the signed, polarity-adjusted Polar waveform, rounded to four decimals in tracking rows. It remains in native signal units, while the separate calibrated LSL output is dimensionless.

  • Target signaltarget_force / target_signal_g

    The generated sinusoidal target uses the study’s calibrated center, amplitude and configured segments, in N for Vernier or g for Polar. It is the reference participants are asked to track, rather than a measured physiological signal.

  • Uncompensated tracking errorerror / error_g

    The target minus the selected input is stored in N for Vernier or g for Polar. This signed difference quantifies tracking in native input units before visual gain is applied.

  • Gain-compensated tracking errorcompensated_error / compensated_error_g

    The target minus the displayed signal, center + gain × (input − center), is stored in N for Vernier or g for Polar. This signed difference describes error under the visual manipulation; trial performance averages its absolute value.

  • Task phasephase

    A categorical string identifies the task stage attached to a sample, such as range_cal, baseline, countdown or tracking. It has no physical unit and separates experimental periods for analysis.

  • Experimental conditioncondition

    A categorical label identifies the configured normal, deep, shallow or corresponding no-feedback condition. It has no physical unit and describes the task manipulation, not an independently measured breathing-depth class.

  • Trial numbertrial_num

    The one-based trial index is recorded as a dimensionless count, with no active trial during session-level calibration. It links sample rows and assessment responses to the configured order.

  • Visual feedback gainfeedback_gain

    The condition’s dimensionless gain scales input deviations about the calibrated center, with 1.0 normal, 0.5 attenuated and 1.5 amplified feedback. This is an experimental parameter rather than a sensor measure, and does not rescale the calibrated LSL channel.

  • Perceived breathing conditionself_condition

    The participant’s key response records n for normal, d for deeper or s for shallower breathing needed to stay on target. It is a categorical, unitless self-report after the trial, distinct from the assigned condition.

  • Judgment confidenceconfidence

    The participant rates confidence in the breathing judgment using a key from 1 (completely unsure) to 5 (sure). It is an ordinal self-report scale without a physical unit.

  • Perceived tracking accuracyself_accuracy

    The participant rates the preceding trial from 1 (very inaccurate) to 5 (very accurate). It is an ordinal, unitless self-report rather than the objective tracking-error measure.

  • Source LSL timelsl_time_s

    Sample rows retain their inlet source timestamp in LSL seconds; assessment rows use response processing time. This monotonic clock links the row to XDF and is independent of the computer timezone.

  • Estimated UTC timeutc_time

    An ISO 8601 UTC timestamp maps the LSL clock to the session’s frozen wall-clock reference. It is an estimate rather than an exact physical acquisition time; clock source, request latency and offset are retained in the time-reference sidecar.

  • Estimated Berlin timeberlin_time

    The same estimated instant is expressed as ISO 8601 Europe/Berlin time, including its UTC offset and seasonal daylight-saving adjustment. UTC remains the unambiguous reference across clock changes.

  • Clock referencetime_reference

    This categorical field identifies the frozen reference as timeapi.io or computer_clock. A setup-time internet request is optional; unavailable or late replies leave the experiment running from the computer clock without waiting at Start.

BIDS physiology adds numeric lsl_time_s, utc_unix_s and berlin_utc_offset_s; event tables retain ISO times and clock provenance. Timestamp definitions and offline behavior.

Final default stream inventory

Our final default batch contains 14 streams: 8 Polar, 2 Vernier and 4 Respyra. All are selected for recording by default when available, even if they are not displayed. With both supported devices streaming and all three inputs usable, the defaults record 14 streams; availability, preferences, optional outputs and other LSL producers can change this count.

Stream nameRecorded contentsLong-form description
Polar_rawECGRecorded contentsRaw ECG, µVLong-form description

The Polar H10 records a single-lead electrocardiogram: the electrical activity of the heart measured at the chest strap. Mini forwards these samples in microvolts, configured at 130 samples per second. This is the original cardiac waveform, allowing later inspection or heartbeat detection rather than relying only on the device’s heart-rate estimate.

Polar_rawACCRecorded contentsThree acceleration axes: X, Y, Z, mgLong-form description

The H10 reports acceleration along three sensor axes in milligravity, configured at 200 samples per second. One stream keeps X, Y and Z together at their original timestamps. The measurements include gravity, posture changes, body movement and breathing-related chest motion. Mini uses these same raw axes to calculate the two chest-motion waveforms below.

Polar_HRRecorded contentsDevice heart rate, bpmLong-form description

This stream contains the heart-rate estimate reported by the H10 over Bluetooth, in beats per minute. Mini forwards the device’s value when it arrives. It does not calculate an additional heart-rate estimate from the exported ECG, and its updates need not occur at the sampling rate of the ECG or accelerometer.

Polar_RRRecorded contentsR–R intervals, msLong-form description

The H10 reports the time between successive cardiac R peaks in milliseconds. Mini forwards each reported interval, preserving the device’s beat-to-beat timing information. Shorter intervals indicate a faster heartbeat. These values support later heart-rate variability analysis, while their irregular arrival reflects individual heartbeats rather than a continuously sampled waveform.

Polar_ChestPCARecorded contentsPrincipal component analysis (PCA) chest-motion waveform, g, plus PCA quality and validityLong-form description

Mini uses principal component analysis (PCA) to learn an axis from chest acceleration. It converts acceleration to g, centers and smooths chest motion, and projects it onto that axis. The signed projection is a breathing-related chest-motion signal and a supported Respyra feedback input. Each row also contains its quality score and processing-validity flag. Its amplitude describes relative motion; it does not directly measure airflow or lung volume.

Polar_ChestDTRecorded contentsDetrended chest-motion waveform, g, plus DT validity, PCA validity and PCA qualityLong-form description

DT means detrended. Mini subtracts each acceleration axis’s rolling 200-second baseline from its short 0.2-second mean, then projects those differences onto the learned PCA axis. This second signed chest-motion candidate gradually removes sustained offsets. Its bundled flags describe both short-window readiness and PCA readiness; they travel with the waveform at the same source timestamp.

Polar_BreathPhaseRecorded contentsEstimated inhale, exhale, pause or unavailable phaseLong-form description

A moving-average classifier assigns a phase to the signed H10 chest-motion signal: +1 for inhalation, −1 for exhalation, 0 for pause and −2 for unavailable or bad signal. This estimated label can support comparisons of breathing phases. Orientation and movement affect interpretation; the label itself is not a continuous waveform used to drive Respyra feedback.

Polar_LinkRecorded contentsTimestamped Bluetooth loss and recovery eventsLong-form description

Polar Mini reports when it detects loss of the Bluetooth device connection, using code 1, and restoration, using code 2. Respyra records these events with their source identity and timestamps. A paired loss and recovery gives an observed outage duration. This housekeeping stream reports connection transitions rather than continuous signal strength or physiological signal quality.

Vernier_rawForceRecorded contentsForce, respiration rate, steps and step rate where supplied, plus acquisition diagnosticsLong-form description

This default raw outlet bundles every numeric measurement exposed by the connected belt. The GDX-RB supplies belt tension in newtons, with firmware respiration rate, step count and step rate when reported. Seven additional channels describe sequence, dropped rows, device drop reports, sample period, decode latency, host receipt time and encoding. Respyra extracts Force from this bundle for feedback.

Vernier_LinkRecorded contentsTimestamped Bluetooth loss and recovery eventsLong-form description

Vernier Mini reports its own Bluetooth connection transitions: code 1 for detected loss and code 2 for restoration. Original event timestamps let researchers relate interruptions to gaps in the belt measurements and calculate paired outage durations. This stream is recorded independently of Polar_Link and may be empty if no connection transition occurs during the recording.

Respyra_rawForce_calibratedRecorded contentsVernier Force channel only, range-calibrated and dimensionlessLong-form description

Respyra extracts Force from Vernier_rawForce and expresses each sample relative to that input’s accepted calibration center and amplitude: (Force − center) / amplitude. The result is dimensionless and retains the original acquisition timestamp. Only the Force channel is transformed into this outlet. Samples are unavailable before calibration; the original full Vernier stream remains recorded alongside it.

Respyra_ChestPCA_calibratedRecorded contentsCalibrated principal component analysis (PCA) waveform, plus its bundled quality/validityLong-form description

Respyra normalizes the Polar_ChestPCA waveform using that input’s own accepted range calibration, yielding a dimensionless chest-motion signal. Its PCA quality and validity channels remain bundled with the valid calibrated rows, using the original source timestamps. The stream is recorded even when not displayed or selected for feedback, enabling later comparison with the other calibrated breathing inputs.

Respyra_ChestDT_calibratedRecorded contentsCalibrated DT waveform, plus its bundled quality/validityLong-form description

Respyra gives Polar_ChestDT a separate range calibration and exports its normalized, dimensionless waveform. DT validity, PCA validity and PCA quality remain attached to valid calibrated rows. Before accepted calibration, values are unavailable; afterward unusable source rows do not produce calibrated samples. Recording this alternative preserves a comparison with PCA and belt Force while only the chosen input controls feedback.

Respyra-EventsRecorded contentsStudy timing, trials, conditions, calibration and recording eventsLong-form description

Respyra publishes timestamped JSON records describing study transitions, trial and condition context, calibration outcomes and recording lifecycle. Their fields provide the experimental context needed to interpret sensor samples and align them with task periods. These events are separate from the two device Link streams. The BIDS export writes study events into the run’s events table for analysis.

The two Link streams are the only separate default device-housekeeping streams. They may contain no samples if no connection transition occurs. Quality and validity are channels within their waveform streams, rather than additional default outlets.

Separate Force copies (Vernier_rawForceOnly), standalone quality/validity streams, All-in-one outputs and additional metrics remain optional and start off. Respyra creates each calibrated copy from its available compatible input; only the selected input drives feedback. Default base names can be renamed in Mini settings; stream metadata and source_id identify the actual producer.

This inventory describes the v2.3.15 suite defaults, including the two Bluetooth housekeeping streams and three calibrated breathing inputs.

Each channel sample also has an LSL/XDF timestamp in seconds, separate from its value. Polar ECG and raw ACC are configured at 130 and 200 Hz; ADR waveforms emit at accepted-notification timing with nominal rate 0 (irregular), and firmware rates/events can be sparse. Combined rows contain NaN for signals that did not update; missing or pre-calibration values are not zero physiology.

Respyra records visible and later-discovered outlets by default, unless their Record boxes are unchecked before Start. Polar_Link and Vernier_Link are visible and recorded directly by default. They emit on connection changes and may be empty during an uninterrupted connection. The selected raw input, selected calibrated output and study events are required. Discovery cannot recover earlier samples. Optional ECG/HRV/coherence features and OS keyboard/mouse hooks are not default selections.

Stream naming rule

Use the shortest unambiguous descriptive name, with established scientific abbreviations where appropriate: ECG, ACC, HR, RR and PCA. Polar and Vernier identify the source; ChestPCA and ChestDT distinguish signed chest-motion waveforms, BreathPhase identifies an estimate, and the two Link streams identify Bluetooth connection diagnostics. These labels do not imply validated airflow or lung-volume measurements.

The updated source uses Polar and Vernier prefixes while retaining source identities, physical units and metric IDs. Calibrated copies replace the source prefix with Respyra and append _calibrated. Custom identifiers follow the source prefix; existing recordings keep their names. Published release names and abbreviation glossary.
